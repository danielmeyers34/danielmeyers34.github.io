What you are looking at

Beefy is a yield-farm manager. You deposit a token; it puts that token to work in a lending market or a trading pool, collects the rewards, sells them, reinvests them, and repeats — keeping a performance fee on each harvest. That fee is not one number: 9.5% on most vaults, 4.5% on a large minority, and a handful sit elsewhere entirely — so the perf fee column reads it per vault from Beefy’s /fees endpoint instead of assuming. It is a separate world from our perp trading: nothing on this page touches our wallets or our bots.

This page ranks every live Beefy vault by where its yield actually comes from. It rebuilds from Beefy's public data every six hours and keeps at most one sample per vault per day. No account, no key, no money at risk.

The one idea that makes every table below readable.

A headline APY blends two things that behave nothing alike:

  • Real yield — money other people pay you: swap fees, loan interest. It keeps paying for as long as the pool is used.
  • Promo yield — freshly minted tokens handed out to attract deposits. It stops when the campaign stops, and most people receiving it sell it.

So a vault paying 9% that is 95% promo is a weaker proposition than one paying 4.7% that is 100% real. The how real column is exactly that ratio — green bar means most of the yield is real.

Each section below opens with one line on what it tells you. The exact formulas are folded into the grey “technical detail” links — open them only if you want them.

🧭 If you ever decide to act on one of these

Copying a contract address into a wallet does not deposit anything — it only makes the token visible. A deposit is two on-chain transactions (approve, then deposit) and is normally done through Beefy's own site with a wallet connected.

  1. The address in each row is the vault contract (Beefy calls it earnContractAddress). Paste it into a block explorer with verify, or into a wallet's “import token” box to watch the receipt token. Never send tokens to it directly.
  2. Check the address against Beefy’s own page first — the open on beefy link goes to that exact vault. If the address on their page differs from the one here, stop; do not proceed on the strength of this page alone.
  3. Getting funds there from our venue is the real cost. Perp collateral is USDC on one chain; almost every vault above is on another. That means withdraw, bridge, and gas at both ends, each way. On a few hundred dollars those costs can exceed a year of the yield — work out the break-even before, not after.
  4. Deposit is approve + deposit on Beefy’s site. You receive a receipt token (a “moo” token) whose value per share grows as the strategy compounds. You get the deposit back by withdrawing on the same site.
  5. What this page cannot tell you: whether the yield survives your holding period, the price move of the underlying, impermanent loss on a two-coin pool, or contract risk. It is a screen, not a diligence report.
Technical detail — how the two yield columns are computed, and the traps
How the two APR columns are defined. fee APR = tradingApr + clmApr + rewardPoolTradingApr + lendingApr — yield paid by counterparties (swappers, borrowers). incentive APR = vaultApr + merklApr + rewardPoolApr + liquidStakingApr + composablePoolApr + lineaIgnitionApr + stellaSwapApr + boost — token issuance and campaigns, which stop when the programme stops. vaultApr is farm emissions, not fees, which is why it sits on the incentive side. The two columns are components: they do not sum to total APY, because Beefy compounds the compoundable parts — totalApy is the authoritative headline.
Fee basis trap: clmApr, vaultApr and lendingApr are already net of the Beefy performance fee; tradingApr, merklApr and the reward-pool APRs are gross. Never re-apply the perf fee to totalApy. A — in the perf/wdr columns means the vault has no /fees row at all (every gov reward pool); it is not a zero. boost APR only accrues if you separately stake the moo token in the boost contract. 7d ΔAPY is computed from this page's own daily samples (the API publishes no APY history), so it reads — until the workflow has run for a week.
What these numbers are not. An advertised APY is a forward extrapolation of a recent window, not a realized return. It excludes impermanent loss, the price move of the underlying, gas, bridging, and smart-contract risk. Stablecoin rows carry depeg risk. Nothing here is a recommendation to deploy capital.

🏦 E · TOKENIZED EQUITIES

Vaults holding a tokenized share — NVIDIA, Microsoft, Apple, Meta, Amazon, Tesla and the like — paired against a stablecoin. 20 vaults, $397k in total, on base, robinhood. This corner behaves differently from the rest of the page: 9 of 20 earn from real trading activity rather than token emissions, because people actually swap these pairs. That is the opposite of what the stablecoin and crypto cards show.

Read the yields here with more suspicion, not less. The fee figures on the concentrated-liquidity rows are a trailing ~1.1-day fee window, annualised. A row reading 200% is one busy day extrapolated across a year, not a rate you can earn — the same vault can read a third of that tomorrow with nothing having changed. Treat these as a measure of how busy the pair was yesterday.
Two hazards specific to tokenized stocks. (1) These are two-sided pools, so if the stock runs you end up holding less of the stock and more of the stablecoin — the fees are paid for out of the upside you were there for. (2) A tokenized share is an issuer's claim, not the share: it adds that issuer’s solvency and redemption terms on top of every other risk on this page. Beefy is not the issuer and does not vouch for the wrapper.
